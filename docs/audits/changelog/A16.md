### Fixed

- **Incremental v1 piece hasher fed at insert (audit A16, round
  2)**: `CachedPieceEntry` now carries a `piece_hasher` (SHA-1)
  consumed block-by-block over the contiguous buffered prefix —
  upstream `kick_hasher`. When the prefix completes, the finalized
  digest is stored on the entry and `force_flush_flag` is raised,
  and `hash` jobs consult the stored digest before touching storage
  (upstream `try_hash_piece`'s `hasher_cursor == blocks_in_piece →
  job_completed` leg): a fully-downloaded piece no longer pays a
  complete disk re-read to produce its piece hash. The cache entry
  is retained until `piece_hash_returned_flag` precisely so the
  digest survives for that lookup — upstream's eviction rule at
  `disk_cache.cpp:996-1006` applies to v1 entries too, not only v2.
  A rewrite below `hasher_cursor` invalidates the stored digest,
  restarts the hasher and recounts `num_unhashed` over the piece
  (upstream asserts the case unreachable; we re-hash gracefully
  rather than answering a stale digest).

- **In-flight flush buffers stay readable (upstream
  `borrowed_buf`)**: `FlushJob` bufs are `Arc` clones of the cache
  block buffers instead of moved-out ownership — between the take
  and `run_flushed` the block keeps serving `get`/`get2`/hash
  consults, closing the stale-read window where an upload could
  fall through to pre-write disk contents. `run_flushed` retires by
  `Arc` identity (`ptr_eq`): a block re-inserted while its flush
  was in flight keeps the newer buffer dirty — and its parked
  writer — for the next pass instead of being clobbered and marked
  flushed. `restore_run` no longer re-installs buffers (they never
  left their slot); it clears the in-flight mark, re-parks the
  returned writer or completes it aborted-superseded when a newer
  write owns the slot.

- **Re-inserts on `flushed` or in-flight blocks**: upstream asserts
  `block_idx >= flushed_cursor` / `>= hasher_cursor`; the graceful
  Rust path now re-dirties a `flushed` block when a rewrite lands
  on it (previously the new buffer was invisible to every flush
  selector — the block would sit cached forever with a writer that
  never completed) and treats a rewrite over an in-flight block as
  a supersede (aborted exactly once, `CACHED` cleared).

- **`CACHED` cleared on out-of-piece write aborts**: a write whose
  block index exceeds `blocks_in_piece` never took a cache slot —
  its orphan now completes aborted with `CACHED` cleared so
  `job_done` releases the `write_blocks` back-pressure charge
  instead of leaking it.

- **`num_unhashed` settles on retire**: `run_flushed` drops the
  contribution of retired blocks still ahead of `hasher_cursor`
  (upstream `m_num_unhashed` tracks *cached* unhashed blocks only),
  so a piece flushed before its hash job runs can no longer leave
  the counter stuck above zero.

- **Fence-parked writes schedule the flush drain**: `async_write`'s
  parked arm now runs the `schedule_flush` equivalent —
  upstream executes `insert_write` → `schedule_flush` even when
  `is_blocked` parks the job, because the parked charge still
  counts toward the global `buffer_level()`. An over-watermark park
  now enqueues drain `Flush` jobs immediately (dirty runs on
  *other* storages included) instead of deferring to the fence's
  repost.

- **Forced pieces drain every dirty run**: `flush_after_insert`
  evaluates `force_flush_flag` before the complete-piece leg and
  takes *all* the piece's dirty runs (`take_all_runs`) like
  upstream's pass-1 iteration over the whole entry — a run split
  by an in-flight gap is no longer left behind until the next
  scheduling event.
